Essential Considerations for Helium Balloons

  1. Helium Balloon Life: Helium balloons naturally deflate over time. Latex balloons usually last 8 to 12 hours, while foil balloons can last several days. If you need them to last longer, you can opt for Hi-Float, a liquid treatment that helps extend the lifespan of latex balloons.
  2. Storage and Weather: Helium balloons are sensitive to temperature changes. They may shrink and deflate faster in cold weather, while in warm temperatures, they can expand and burst. It’s best to keep them indoors or in controlled conditions, significantly if they’re inflated a day before the event.
  3. Safety: Always ensure that balloons are tied securely to prevent them from floating away, especially if you are outdoors. Ensure children are supervised, as the rubber can be a choking hazard if popped.

Latex helium balloons typically last 8-12 hours before starting to deflate. Factors like temperature and the balloon’s size can affect this time.

To tie helium balloons, gather the neck of the balloon, twist it to seal the opening, and tie a knot at the base of the neck. For added security, you can use a ribbon or balloon weight.

While it’s not ideal to reuse helium balloons, they can sometimes be refilled with helium if they haven’t lost their shape or material integrity.

To store helium balloons overnight, keep them in a cool, dry area away from heat sources. It’s best not to leave them in direct sunlight, as the helium can expand and cause the balloons to burst.

Helium balloons will not float without helium. However, you can use air-filled balloons for decoration, though they will not float.
